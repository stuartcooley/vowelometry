# Vowelometry

**Invented by Stuart Cooley, March 31, 2026**
*A tonal calibration system for literary prose*

---

## What is Vowelometry?

Vowelometry is a system for measuring and steering the emotional tone of written 
prose through the deliberate density and weighting of vowel sounds. It was invented 
by Stuart Cooley in March 2026 as part of the architecture of his memoir, 
*Still In It*.

The core insight: vowel sounds carry emotional register independent of word meaning. 
By tracking which vowel phonemes dominate a passage, a writer can diagnose tone 
and steer it deliberately — from grief to irony to absurdity — without losing 
their natural voice.

---

## The Core Unit

**V=** (vowel sounds per 10 tokens), calibrated for *Still In It*.

The short-I (ɪ) — as in *still*, *in*, *it* — is the sound of persistence.
It is the spine of the memoir and carries the highest phoneme weight in this system.

---

## The 20-Tone Scale

Vowelometry maps 20 distinct tonal registers to V= targets, from:

- **Absurdity (V=37)** — sparse, schwa-dominant, flat
- up through Grief, Wonder, Indignation, Regret, Sardonic
- to **Irony (V=92)** — dense ɪ + eɪ mix, crisp consonants

Vowelometry was developed as part of **PVGURU** — the working codename for 
*Still In It*, a memoir by Stuart Cooley. The system governs tonal calibration 
across all 23 chapters of the book.
